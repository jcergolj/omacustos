    Both reviews covered the combined seven-child implementation before the closure commit. Final correctness/performance evidence is also recorded on #63; this report is delivered by its issue-closing commit on master.

    Performance evidence¶

    The final local measurements contain 18 successful invocations: two repeats for three profiles at three archive targets, including v3/v4 baseline, unchanged, edit, full/selective restore, cleanup, and retained-state restore. On 2026-10-21, the closure check independently parsed all 252 JSON records in the 18 local raw evidence files and reproduced their documented receipt SHA-256:

    8e14a0fe161d0919f99280aee3f7fb93839b03c3fd0117d59426e7040e84ea7e

    Raw files remain local at /tmp/opencode/issue62-final-20261021; the linked report preserves the results and measurement boundaries in the repository. At the retained 1,000 MB requested archive target, the 512 MiB amplified-code fixture took 1.856–1.936 s for unchanged v4 checks versus 5.066–5.394 s for v3; the mixed fixture took 2.296–2.465 s versus 10.687–10.757 s. Unchanged runs uploaded no payload objects and created no snapshot. Small edits uploaded one whole changed file. New baselines were slower, and fixed overhead made the small unamplified repository fixture slower with v4. These are local-provider results, not measured cloud speedups.

    The approximately 1 GB requested target, existing 1 GB staging budget, and 1,000-file cap remain unchanged. Smaller targets reduced selective transfer but increased remote operations; the linked report records the measured tradeoff. 20/50/100/500 GB fixtures were impractical under the available disk capacity and the conservative fixture guard. No cloud measurements were performed.

    Preserved limits and safeguards¶

    • All selected regular files are hashed; capture is file-by-file, not an atomic application/filesystem snapshot. Changed files are transferred whole.
    • New uploads are fully verified. Daily reuse checks backed by original verification evidence can miss same-sized remote corruption; scheduled full integrity checks and verified restores remain necessary.
    • Each saved state is independently restorable, including legacy history. Shared physical membership remains distinct from logical snapshot membership.
    • Ownership takeover is explicit and manual-offline: the previous writer must be permanently stopped. This is not server-enforced fencing.
    • Safe cleanup requires exact-scope confirmation, retains newest-N saved states across formats, and protects retained/resumable references. Unchanged checks consume no slot. Retained history is never removed to make upload space.
    • The first new-format run uploads a fresh baseline with capacity estimates. Replacing damaged objects for a new state does not repair older snapshots.
    • Partially live archives keep obsolete members; compaction and block-level deduplication remain deferred.
